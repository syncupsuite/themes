# Nihon no Iro — Traditional Japanese Colors

> 日本の伝統色 — Colors drawn from nature, seasons, and centuries of aesthetic refinement.

## Story

Japan's traditional color vocabulary spans over 1,000 named hues, each tied to a natural phenomenon, season, or material. These colors emerged through centuries of textile dyeing, ceramics, and woodblock printing. The palette embodies **wabi-sabi** (侘寂) — finding beauty in imperfection, impermanence, and depth.

## Seed Colors

| Color | Name | Hex | Origin |
|-------|------|-----|--------|
| Hanada (縹) | Indigo blue | `#2E4B6D` | Ai-zome indigo dyeing tradition |
| Sakura-nezumi (桜鼠) | Cherry-blossom grey | `#B5A4A0` | Edo-period kimono textiles |
| Wasurenagusa-iro (勿忘草色) | Forget-me-not | `#7BAACC` | Meiji-era botanical illustration |
| Kitsune-iro (狐色) | Fox color | `#C6A96C` | Lacquerware and ceramic glazes |
| Enji (臙脂) | Cochineal crimson | `#9B3B47` | Chinese-trade court garments |
| Rikyuu-nezumi (利休鼠) | Tea-master grey | `#7A7D74` | Sen no Rikyuu tea ceremony |
| Shironeri (白練) | Refined silk white | `#F3ECDC` | Raw silk after degumming |

## Design Characteristics

- **Harmony mode**: Golden ratio — hues spaced by the golden angle
- **Typography**: Humanist serif (`"Noto Serif JP"`)
- **Border radius**: Subtle (2-6px)
- **Neutrals**: Warm-tinted, drawn from Hanada hue
